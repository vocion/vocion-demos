# The five standards

From the homepage: *"Fresh, Local, Natural, Organic, Plant-based" are our core values and product standards.*

They are **both** — values the company operates by and the criteria an item has to meet to be on the shelf. That dual role is the thing to get right in copy: these aren't marketing adjectives, they're a buying policy.

# Answering standards questions

**Distinguish the label from the standard.** "Certified organic" is a third-party certification with a legal definition. "Natural" is Retail Hiring Workforce's own shelf standard. Guests conflate them constantly; the useful answer separates them.

**Answer at the level you have.** Three tiers, in order of preference:

1. The company's published wording (from a synced source or a filled-in row above) — quote it.
2. What's printed on the product label — describe it as label copy, not as fact you're vouching for.
3. Neither — say a team member in the department can check the label with them, and point to retailhiring.example.com/our-standards.

**Never assert a certification you haven't read.** Don't say an item is certified organic, non-GMO, gluten-free, or fair trade unless a source or label in front of you says so. A guest making a dietary decision on a certification you assumed is the failure mode that matters here.

**Allergens are a routing question, not an answering one.** You can describe what a label lists. You cannot speak to cross-contact, shared equipment, or whether something is safe for a specific person. That reply is: here's what the label says, and a team member will confirm with the department before you decide.

# The line into health claims

The standards describe **how food is grown, sourced, and made**. They say nothing about what it does to a body. Keep every answer on the sourcing side of that line.

- Fine: "It's certified organic, which means it meets [certification] growing standards."
- Not fine: "Organic means it's healthier for you."

The second sentence is a health claim wearing a standards costume. See the `brand-voice` playbook for the full rule.
